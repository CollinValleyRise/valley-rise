What I look for

The specifics vary by business. These are the things I look at first.

Profitable and well run

A track record of steady profits and clean financials.

Customers who come back

Repeat relationships, without heavy dependence on a few accounts.

A lasting position

A business known and trusted in its market, with a team that can run it day to day.

Ways this can work

These are the structures I'm open to discussing.

i.

A clean exit

You step away at close, with a short, defined handoff.

ii.

A planned handoff

You stay on for three to twelve months while I learn the business, your customers, and your team.

How you get paid

Most of the purchase price is paid in cash at closing, funded by my investors' equity and debt financing. We agree on price and structure in a letter of intent before diligence begins.

How it works

Four steps, on a timeline we set together.

i.

Intro call

A short, confidential conversation to see whether we're a fit, with no obligation on either side.

ii.

Agree on price, terms, and your transition

A letter of intent sets the price, the structure, and how long you stay involved.

iii.

Diligence

We sign an NDA first, then I review the business with as little disruption to your team as possible.

iv.

Close and handoff

I take over day to day as CEO and work with you through the agreed transition.

Common questions

Who are you, and how is this different from a private equity firm?

Unlike a traditional private equity firm that manages dozens of companies with a team of financial analysts, I am an individual entrepreneur backed by experienced institutional investors. I am looking to buy one great business and dedicate 100% of my time to running it day-to-day as CEO. I focus on long-term sustainability rather than quick financial engineering.

What happens to my employees?

I'm buying this business because of how it runs today. Your team knows it best, and I plan to rely on them.

Who is backing you?

Experienced search fund investors have committed capital to help me acquire one company. Financing for a deal combines their equity with debt. I'm glad to go through the details with you.

Are you willing to relocate to run my business?

Absolutely. Once we agree to terms and close on the business, my family and I will relocate directly to your community. I am fully committed to being on the ground every day, leading from the front.

What does the transition process look like for me as an owner?

The transition is completely structured around your goals. While I will take over day-to-day CEO responsibilities from day one, we will design a transition window (typically 3 to 12 months) where you stay on in an advisory role to train me on key operational nuances, customer relationships, and vendor dynamics.

How do we keep our conversations confidential?

We execute a standard mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) before sharing any sensitive business details. I respect the quiet operations of your business, and our discussions remain completely off the radar from your employees, competitors, and customers until you are ready to announce a deal.

How long does the sale process take from start to finish?

Once we sign an initial Letter of Intent (LOI), a typical transaction takes 60 to 90 days to complete full due diligence, finalize legal documents, and close. My goal is to work efficiently and transparently so you can keep 100% of your focus on running the business during the process.

How do you value my business?

Valuation is primarily based on a multiple of your historical cash flow (typically via EBITDA). However, every business is unique. I take into account your company's growth trajectory, customer retention, market position, and operational strength to structure a fair, competitive offer that reflects the true value of what you've built.

What will week one look like after the deal closes?

Day one is about listening, learning, and building trust. I won't come in making sweeping operational changes. My immediate priority is to meet with employees, introduce myself, reassure them about job security, and listen to their feedback. The first 90 days are dedicated to learning the business from the inside out alongside your leadership team.
